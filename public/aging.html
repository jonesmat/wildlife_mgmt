Buck Watch Home
Estimate a whitetail's age class two ways: on the hoof from body shape before the shot, or from the jawbone (tooth replacement and wear) after harvest. Pick buck or doe, then answer what you can see — the estimate updates as you go.

Method
Sources. On-the-hoof cues are adapted from E. R. Fuchs, Aging on the Hoof (Kerr Wildlife Management Area, Texas Parks and Wildlife) and S. Demarais, D. Stewart & R. N. Griffin, Hunter's Guide to Aging and Judging Live White-Tailed Deer in the Southeast (Mississippi State University Extension P2206). The jawbone key follows A. Cain & M. Wallace, A Guide to Age Determination of White-tailed Deer (Texas Parks and Wildlife, PWD BK W7000-755). Aging on the hoof is an art, not a science — body condition, time of year, protein supplements and region all shift the look, and no two deer are alike. Tooth wear varies with soil and diet; check both sides of the jaw.
