How a spotlight census year goes
July: set upPick a 2–15 mile route away from feeders. Measure visibility every 1/10 mile, left and right, with your count light.
Aug to early Oct: countAt least 3 counts on separate nights, about a week apart. Start after dark and drive 5–8 mph. Tally bucks, does, fawns and unknowns.
October: decidePool the counts into acres per deer, a herd estimate and ratios. Set the season's doe and buck harvest.
Nov to Jan: harvestRecord age, weight and antlers on every deer taken. Harvest data checks the census.

Visibility

Stop at each reading point and record how far, in yards, your light shows a deer on each side. Measure once in July with the same light. Measure again only after brush work or a burn changes the cover.
How to measure how far you can see

At each stop, record how far out you could still spot eyeshine and tell a buck from a doe. That's often closer than where the beam fades out.

  • Laser rangefinder at night (best). Shine your count light straight out to the side and range the farthest spot a deer would still show: the brush line, a tree edge, or the top of a rise.
  • Open pasture with nothing to range: use your light's useful distance and keep that same number every year. Set it once by ranging a post or a truck at the edge of where you can still tell sex.
  • Satellite map as a cross-check. Measure from the road to the tree line at each stop. This works for clean pasture edges but reads too far in brush.
  • By eye, in 25-yard steps. Practice first against known distances. It's less exact, but fine if it's done the same way every year.

Rules that matter more than precision:

  • Measure at night with the light you count with. Your daytime view is much farther.
  • Enter 0 where the view is blocked by a cut bank, wall of brush or creek bank. Don't skip the stop.
  • Measure in Aug–Sep with the leaves on. Winter readings run long and make the density look too low.
  • Use the same light, vehicle and seat height every time. A spotter standing in a truck bed sees farther than one in the cab.

A 2-mile route at 1/10-mile stops is 21 stops: about an hour with a driver and someone on the rangefinder.
